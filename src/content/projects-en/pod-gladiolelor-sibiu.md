The structure is located within the built-up area of Sibiu, in the Terezian district, at the end of Gladiolelor Street, between Cibinului Street and Râului Street.

## Project context

The bridge on Gladiolelor Street, also known as the Maria Tereza Bridge, lies within the built-up area of Sibiu Municipality, in the Terezian district, at the end of Gladiolelor Street, between Cibinului Street and Râului Street.

## What a major repair involves

Unlike routine maintenance, a major repair works on the structural elements and has to go through the full design and permitting process. The documentation is based on the structural assessment, which establishes the actual condition of the structure and the admissible intervention solutions.

## Urban and heritage context

Being located in a historic part of Sibiu adds two kinds of constraints:

- **Heritage**: the solutions must be compatible with the cultural value of the structure, and the documentation goes through additional approvals
- **Construction**: in a residential district, site set-up, traffic diversions and access for residents dictate how the works are staged

These constraints are dealt with at the design stage, not during construction. A solution that is technically correct but cannot be built without closing the road for a long time is not a solution.

## Documentation prepared

The project was prepared in the building permit design (DTAC) and detailed technical design (PTE) phases, for Sibiu Municipality.

## What it brings to the community

The bridge serves the Terezian district, a residential area of Sibiu with historic value:

- **Keeping the link** between Cibinului Street and Râului Street, for the residents who use it every day
- **Traffic safety**, through work on the affected structural elements
- **Preserving the character of the area**, with solutions compatible with the heritage value of the structure
- **Avoiding a long closure**, through staging planned at the design stage

## Improvements on site

The major repair works on the structural elements, based on the structural assessment that established the actual condition of the structure. In a residential district, site set-up and traffic diversions were treated as part of the solution, not as a problem to be solved during construction, so that access for residents is disrupted as little as possible.
