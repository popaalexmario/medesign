Design of riverbank works with retaining walls, sidewalks and footbridges for continuity of movement.

## Project background

The green-blue infrastructure project in Bughea de Jos commune, Argeș County, brought together in a single set of documents components that are usually handled separately: riverbank works, retaining structures, pedestrian circulation and public space.

## The designed solution

The documentation covered:

- Retaining walls for shaping and stabilising the banks
- Sidewalks for safe pedestrian movement
- Footbridges that keep movement continuous across the watercourse
- Landscaping of the adjacent public space

## Why the integrated approach mattered

In a project like this, the hydraulic component drives everything else. The bank finished levels, the sizing of the retaining walls and the position of the footbridges all depend on the flow regime. A solution that treats the landscaping separately from the hydraulic works usually turns out not to work at the first flood.

We went through three stages: the feasibility study (SF), the building permit design (DTAC) and the detailed technical design (PTE). Since these were new structures located in relation to a watercourse, classing the documentation as a feasibility study was the correct choice.

## What it brings to the community

The works answer a problem felt directly by the people living near the watercourse:

- Protection of the households on the banks against erosion and flood effects
- Continuous pedestrian movement over the footbridges, where the watercourse used to split up journeys
- Usable public space, in a commune where this kind of amenity is usually missing
- A link between the parts of the village separated by the riverbed, without detours

## Improvements on site

The banks get a retaining structure that stops progressive erosion. The sidewalks bring pedestrian movement separated from the carriageway, and the footbridges create permanent crossings, usable whatever the water level. The green space component turns a technical area into one the community can use, a combination rarely seen at the scale of a commune.
