Design of 12.15 km of county road with two bridges and embankment stabilisation.

## Project background

The upgrading and rehabilitation of county road DJ 101S, for Prahova County Council, covered **12.15 km of county road**, including two bridges and embankment stabilisation works.

## Why they cannot be treated separately

A county road of this length is not a uniform job. Along the route there is a succession of sections with different problems: stretches where the pavement structure is the main deficiency, stretches where the embankment is unstable, and the points where the bridges and other structures drive the whole solution.

Treating the three components (road, bridges, stabilisation) separately would have led to solutions that do not fit together. A bridge rehabilitated at a level that does not match the road's longitudinal profile, or a stabilisation designed without considering the loads from the final pavement structure, creates additional work during construction.

## Documentation produced

The project was prepared at the intervention approval documentation stage (DALI), with the related technical expertise and site investigations, treating the three categories of works as a whole. For the existing bridges, the assessment of their technical condition determined how the intervention was classed and which solutions were admissible.

## What it brings to the community

A 12.15 km county road links villages that depend on it for almost every journey:

- Access to services in the county seat: medical, administrative, educational
- A continuous link, secured by rehabilitating the two bridges on the route, where losing either would have meant detours of tens of kilometres
- Traffic safety on the sections where embankment instability was a risk
- Conditions for local economic activity, which depends on transport along this route

## Improvements on site

The works treat the three components as a whole: the pavement structure over the full length, the two bridges and the embankment stabilisation in the unstable zones. Tying the bridges into the road's longitudinal profile and matching the stabilisation to the loads from the final structure removes the additional work that usually appears when the components are designed separately.
